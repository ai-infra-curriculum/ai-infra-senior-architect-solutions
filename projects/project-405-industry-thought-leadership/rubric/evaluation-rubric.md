# Evaluation Rubric — Industry Thought Leadership Programme

> Grading rubric for learner submissions to Project 405. The
> rubric weights **defensibility of the programme thesis** and
> **enforceability of the review workflow** above **breadth of
> external activity**. A learner who submits a plan for fifty
> speaking engagements per year with no review workflow will
> score below a learner who submits ten defended engagements
> per year and a working review workflow.

**Scoring model.** Six weighted dimensions, 0–100 within each,
then weighted-summed to a final 0–100 programme score. Two
dimensions are non-passing thresholds: a submission scoring
below 60 on either Dimension 1 (programme thesis) or Dimension
2 (review workflow) is non-passing regardless of other scores.

**Grader posture.** The rubric is written for a reviewer who
would sit on the External Voice Review Board or the Board
Strategy Committee — someone who is testing whether they could
operate this programme if it were their own, not whether the
submission looks impressive.

---

## Dimension 1 — Programme thesis defensibility (weight: 20%)

**What the grader is testing.** Is there a written thesis, does
the thesis exclude conversations and audiences as much as it
includes them, and could a Board Strategy Committee member
redirect the programme by changing the thesis?

### 1.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Thesis is written in three sentences, names the conversations Halcyon is trying to influence, names the primary audiences, and states what is deliberately not chased. Excludes named alternatives (general AI ethics discourse, consumer-AI news cycles, cross-industry AGI debate, retail-investor audiences) with argued reasons. Programme decisions can be traced to the thesis. |
| **75–89** | Thesis is written and scoped. Excludes some alternatives but not all obvious ones. Programme decisions mostly trace to the thesis; some are unattached. |
| **60–74** | Thesis is present but reads as a mission statement — accumulates consensus adjectives, does not exclude alternatives explicitly. Programme decisions are argued but not from the thesis. |
| **40–59** | Thesis is fragmentary or aspirational. Reader cannot tell what the programme is *not* doing. |
| **0–39** | No written thesis, or thesis is a mission statement with no scoping content. |

### 1.2 Grader questions

- Can the thesis be quoted in three sentences?
- Does the thesis name the primary audiences and the primary
  conversations, with argued exclusions?
- If a Board member wanted to redirect the programme, would
  they change the thesis or the activity list?
- Are exclusions defended, or hand-waved?

### 1.3 Common down-marks

- Twelve-adjective mission statement (down-mark to at most 60).
- No exclusions named (down-mark to at most 65).
- Thesis exists but activity decisions do not reference it
  (down-mark to at most 70).

**Non-passing threshold: below 60 fails this dimension and the
overall submission regardless of other scores.**

---

## Dimension 2 — Review workflow enforceability (weight: 20%)

**What the grader is testing.** Is "reviewed at the correct
tier with reviewers on file" the definition of published, and
does the submission produce an auditable archive as evidence?

### 2.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Review workflow tiered proportional to risk (four tiers). Latency targets published per tier. Reviewer identities recorded per artifact. Confidential-material handling escalates to a defined higher tier. Emergency (Tier D) path exists on an on-call rota. Archive is auditable in under fifteen minutes per artifact. Missing sign-off blocks publication. |
| **75–89** | Tiered workflow exists and covers most surfaces. Latency targets exist but some are informal. Archive is present but audit path is under-specified. |
| **60–74** | Workflow described in prose. Tiers exist but are loosely defined. Reviewer records are informal. Emergency path is unclear. |
| **40–59** | Workflow is described as intention. "Reviewed" is a self-assessment; no archive discipline. |
| **0–39** | No review workflow. Publication is at the submitter's discretion. |

### 2.2 Grader questions

- Are the tiers named and their latencies defined?
- Where is the archive?
- Can the submission produce a reviewed artifact-of-record for a
  worked example?
- What is the emergency (Tier D) path, and who is on the rota?
- What triggers automatic escalation to a higher tier?

### 2.3 Common down-marks

- One-tier ("everything goes through Communications") workflow
  (down-mark to at most 65) — either too slow for routine
  work or too permissive for high-risk positions.
- No emergency path (down-mark to at most 70).
- No archival discipline (down-mark to at most 65).
- Confidential-material handling not defined (down-mark to at
  most 70).

**Non-passing threshold: below 60 fails this dimension and the
overall submission regardless of other scores.**

## Dimension 3 — Standards-participation model (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Is standards-body membership
argued and time-boxed, with named delegates, prepared positions,
and defined IP posture?

### 3.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Active membership list is named with argued reasons per body. Non-membership decisions have archived arguments. Every body has a named delegate with a named successor. Delegates prepare positions in writing pre-meeting; positions archived post-meeting. IP posture per body is defined in writing (RAND / FRAND declaration, non-assertion covenants, cross-licensing). Exit criterion per body is named. |
| **75–89** | Membership list and IP posture defined. Named delegates exist. Some elements (successor, prepared positions, exit criterion) partial. |
| **60–74** | Membership described in prose. Delegates named but no rotation or successor policy. IP posture not per-body. |
| **40–59** | Membership is opportunistic; no argued list, no delegate policy. |
| **0–39** | No standards-participation model. |

### 3.2 Grader questions

- Can the grader find the argued reason for each membership?
- What happens when a named delegate leaves Halcyon?
- Where is the IP posture per body?
- What is the exit criterion for a body Halcyon is currently a
  member of?

### 3.3 Common down-marks

- No named successor per delegate (down-mark to at most 70).
- IP posture defined only at the programme level, not per body
  (down-mark to at most 75).
- No archived arguments for non-membership decisions (down-mark
  to at most 75).
- Membership by inertia — bodies with no argued reason (down-mark
  to at most 65).

## Dimension 4 — Analyst and executive-briefing model (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Are analysts tiered with a
briefing cadence and a Halcyon-owned content pipeline? Does the
Executive Briefing Centre run against a published curriculum
with a rotating executive sponsor?

### 4.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Analyst tiering defined (Tiers 1–3 with cadence). Named analyst list per tier. Prepared narrative per cycle, reviewed. Paid-research authorisation is batch and argued, separated from briefings and speaking. Executive Briefing Centre curriculum published with named modules, owners, and review cadences. Intake criteria defined (customer strategic importance, AI adoption horizon, specific question). Executive-sponsor rotation on 18-month cadence. Post-briefing hand-off to sales defined. |
| **75–89** | Tiering and curriculum exist. Rotation and hand-off partly defined. Paid-research posture stated but not batch-authorised. |
| **60–74** | Analyst relations described as an activity; no cadence discipline. Executive Briefing Centre operates without a published curriculum. |
| **40–59** | Analyst relations is damage-control-only. Executive Briefing Centre is one executive's slide deck. |
| **0–39** | No analyst-relations model; no Executive Briefing Centre. |

### 4.2 Grader questions

- What is the briefing cadence for a named Tier 1 analyst?
- Who owns each Executive Briefing Centre curriculum module?
- What is the executive-sponsor rotation cadence?
- How does the submission distinguish briefings from paid
  research?

### 4.3 Common down-marks

- Single-executive-sponsor Executive Briefing Centre (down-mark
  to at most 70).
- Blurred paid-research / briefings posture (down-mark to at
  most 65).
- No curriculum ownership (down-mark to at most 70).
- Analyst relations without a tiering (down-mark to at most 70).

## Dimension 5 — Metrics resistance to gaming (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Do the headline metrics reward
influence outcomes (standards positions adopted, analyst-report
placements, hiring signal, executive-briefing conversion,
review-workflow adherence) sourced outside the programme? Are
gameable metrics (impressions, followers, keynote counts) kept
off the headline?

### 5.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Small headline scorecard (five or fewer). Standards positions adopted, analyst-report placement outcomes, hiring signal, executive-briefing conversion, review-workflow adherence — or defensible equivalents. Sources are outside the programme (HR attribution, sales-operations attribution, standards-body records, analyst-firm reports, review archive audit). Vanity metrics (impressions, followers, PR mentions, keynote count, open-source PR count) explicitly refused at headline; reported as hygiene at most. Definition change is a governance artifact. |
| **75–89** | Scorecard is small and influence-oriented. One or two gameable metrics still on headline. |
| **60–74** | Scorecard mixes influence and activity metrics. Gaming resistance not explicit. |
| **40–59** | Scorecard is activity-count-heavy — impressions, keynotes, mentions lead. |
| **0–39** | No scorecard, or scorecard is "leading indicators" of activity. |

### 5.2 Grader questions

- What does the scorecard measure that is sourced outside the
  programme?
- What is deliberately not counted, and why?
- Who owns the definition of each metric?
- What happens when a metric drifts (misses two consecutive
  quarters)?

### 5.3 Common down-marks

- Impressions or follower count as headline (down-mark to at
  most 60).
- Keynote count as headline (down-mark to at most 65).
- Press mention count as headline (down-mark to at most 65).
- Programme scores its own outcomes (down-mark to at most 70).
- Scorecard exceeds seven top-line metrics (down-mark to at
  most 75).

## Dimension 6 — External Voice Review Board governance (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Is the External Voice Review
Board chartered with scoped, binding authority on the classes
named, with dissent recorded and an emergency path?

### 6.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | EVRB chartered as an artifact. Composition fixed-role. Binding authority scoped (tier assignment, Tier B / C / D approvals, standards-body entry/exit, EBC curriculum, analyst tiering, paid research, above-threshold community contributions). Advisory-only classes named (individual technical content in a Distinguished voice's domain, personal-channel content, press-crisis tactics). Explicitly-out-of-scope classes named. Quorum rules defined per tier. Dissent recorded with thresholds. Escalation to Board Strategy Committee defined. Conflicts-of-interest policy standing and per-decision. Annual charter review. |
| **75–89** | Board chartered; composition and authority mostly defined. Dissent recorded but thresholds informal. |
| **60–74** | Board described but as "editorial committee" — advises, does not decide. Authority not scoped. |
| **40–59** | Board is aspirational — meets when it can, no authority defined. |
| **0–39** | No governance body. Communications is the decision-maker by default; loudest voice wins in practice. |

### 6.2 Grader questions

- Is the Board binding on anything?
- Is dissent recorded?
- What is the emergency (Tier D) path?
- What happens if the Board disagrees with an executive voice?
- How are conflicts of interest handled?

### 6.3 Common down-marks

- Editorial-committee framing (down-mark to at most 65).
- No dissent recording (down-mark to at most 65).
- No emergency path (down-mark to at most 70).
- No conflicts-of-interest policy (down-mark to at most 70).
- No advisory / out-of-scope distinction (down-mark to at most 75).

## Dimension weights and aggregation

| # | Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Programme thesis defensibility | 20% |
| 2 | Review workflow enforceability | 20% |
| 3 | Standards-participation model | 15% |
| 4 | Analyst and executive-briefing model | 15% |
| 5 | Metrics resistance to gaming | 15% |
| 6 | External Voice Review Board governance | 15% |
| **Total** | | **100%** |

**Aggregation.** Weighted sum of the six dimension scores.

**Overall bands.**

| Overall score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Ready for Board Strategy Committee ratification as-is. |
| **80–89** | Ready with minor edits; a senior architect would sign it out after one review pass. |
| **70–79** | Solid draft; a second review pass required. Named gaps but no structural failures. |
| **60–69** | Directional but structurally incomplete. Requires a rewrite of one or two dimensions. |
| **Below 60** | Fundamentally not ready. Rework required. |

**Non-passing thresholds.** A submission scoring below 60 on
either Dimension 1 or Dimension 2 fails the assessment overall,
regardless of scores on the other dimensions. A senior architect
who cannot show either "what we are trying to influence" or
"how content actually gets reviewed" has not demonstrated the
level.

## Grader notes

### What the rubric is not

- **It is not a scoring key.** A submission that structures its
  programme differently but defends the same properties can
  score at the top band. For example, a submission with three
  review tiers rather than four can score full marks on
  Dimension 2 if its tiering defends the three-tier choice
  against four.
- **It is not a checklist for the reference programme.** A
  submission that reproduces the reference programme verbatim,
  without argued adaptation to its own scenario, scores lower
  than one that adapts sensibly.
- **It is not a preference for length.** Concise programmes
  that answer the six dimensions score above verbose
  programmes that hedge on all six.

### Reviewer discipline

- Grade against the rubric, not against personal preference for
  a specific communications-agency methodology or influencer-
  marketing framework.
- Distinguish "I would have done this differently" from "this
  is wrong". The former is a note; the latter is a down-mark.
- Where a submission adapts a reference component to its
  scenario, credit the adaptation.
- Where a submission omits a component the scenario would
  require (for example, an analyst-tiering model at a
  company whose buyers consume analyst reports), down-mark
  rather than making the case for the learner.

### What to write in the review

- One paragraph per dimension: score, one sentence explaining
  the score, one to two sentences on the strongest and
  weakest points.
- Explicit call-out of any non-passing threshold hit.
- Named recommended next-review items in priority order.
- Do not rewrite the learner's programme in the review; the
  review is grader feedback, not co-authoring.
