# External Voice Review Board — Charter

> Reference charter for the Halcyon External Voice Review Board
> (EVRB). Read alongside `../SOLUTION.md` and
> `../program/thought-leadership-program-design.md`. The
> programme depends on this Board being able to say "no" to
> named voices proposing to publish on programme surfaces
> without carrying reputational cost personally. This charter
> is designed to give the Board that authority in scoped,
> defensible form.

**Version**: 1.0
**Status**: Reference charter
**Owner**: Board Strategy Committee (charter ratification);
Chief Marketing Officer (operational chair)

---

## 1. Purpose

The External Voice Review Board governs Halcyon's external
voice on AI on programme surfaces (see `../SOLUTION.md` §2.2).
Its purpose is to make and record decisions about what Halcyon
publishes, on which surfaces, under which name, at which review
tier — and to protect the programme from three failure modes
that enterprise external-voice programmes are documented to
fail into:

- **Drift** — the review workflow quietly loosens as friction
  complaints accumulate and no incidents occur, until an
  incident does occur and the workflow's protective properties
  are absent.
- **Capture** — the Board defaults to approving whatever a
  senior executive proposes, because no reviewer wants to be
  the one who told the CTO the position is off-thesis.
- **Theatre** — the programme reports high activity across all
  surfaces because activity is measurable, while the strategic
  question of "which conversations are we actually
  influencing" goes unanswered.

The EVRB is designed against those failure modes explicitly.
Its authority is scoped so it is neither an editorial rubber
stamp nor a hostile gatekeeper: it approves at Tier A and B in
quantity, at Tier C with argument, and at Tier D at speed.

## 2. Composition

### 2.1 Fixed-role seats

The EVRB has the following fixed seats. Each seat is filled by
a role, not a person; when the incumbent rotates, the seat
transfers.

| Seat | Role |
|---|---|
| Chair | Chief Marketing Officer or delegate at the CMO's level |
| Legal | General Counsel or delegate (typically the Head of IP for filings-adjacent content, the Head of Regulatory for regulator-adjacent content, or the Head of Litigation for competitor-adjacent content, per meeting agenda) |
| Security | Chief Information Security Officer or delegate at that level |
| Technical (senior) | Chief AI Scientist or Distinguished-level equivalent |
| Product | Chief Product Officer or delegate at that level |
| Lab | AI Lab Director (Project 404 interface) |
| Communications operator | Head of Communications operations. Executes the workflow; is a full voting member on operational decisions and a non-voting participant on technical positions |
| Board Strategy Committee liaison | Attends by invitation for Tier C decisions and standing quarterly review; non-voting |

### 2.2 Standing invitees (non-voting)

The following are standing invitees. They attend, contribute
to discussion, and do not vote:

- Head of Analyst Relations — when analyst-relations items are
  on the agenda.
- Executive Briefing Centre Manager — when Executive Briefing
  Centre curriculum items are on the agenda.
- Head of Open-Source Programme Office (OSPO) — when
  community and open-source items are on the agenda.
- Head of Regulatory Affairs — when regulatory-consultation
  content is on the agenda.
- Head of Investor Relations — when investor-facing AI
  content is on the agenda.

### 2.3 Independence

The EVRB does not require independent members in the sense of
the Innovation Portfolio Board (which handles third-party
research spend). The EVRB adjudicates Halcyon-internal
publication decisions; independent review of the programme's
performance is done annually by the external programme review
described in `../program/thought-leadership-program-design.md`
§9 and §11.

### 2.4 Term and rotation

- Chair: no fixed term; rotates with the CMO role.
- Fixed-role seats: rotate with the underlying role.
- Communications operator: no fixed term; rotates on
  organisational change within Communications.
- Board Strategy Committee liaison: rotates with the Committee's
  own rotation schedule.

## 3. Authority

### 3.1 Binding decisions

The EVRB is binding on:

- **Review-tier assignment.** For any submission where the
  tier is ambiguous, the Board sets the tier. Content
  submitters may not self-assign a lower tier than the Board
  has ruled applies to the content class.
- **Tier B approvals.** Tier B approvals require the four
  Review Board seats (Communications, Legal, Security,
  relevant technical voice) to sign off. Absence of any
  required sign-off blocks publication.
- **Tier C approvals.** Tier C approvals require the full
  Board to convene and record the decision, including
  dissent. Board Strategy Committee liaison acknowledgement
  is required.
- **Tier D approvals.** Tier D approvals are made by the on-
  call subset per the emergency rota; General Counsel and
  Communications lead are non-optional. Post-hoc archival
  and full-Board notification within five business days.
- **Standards-body membership entry and exit.** Decisions to
  join or leave a standards body pass through the Board with
  the argued-membership template (`../program/...` Appendix
  A) attached.
- **Standards-delegate nomination and rotation.** Delegates
  are proposed by the AI Lab Director / Chief AI Scientist;
  the Board approves against the delegate policy.
- **Executive Briefing Centre curriculum changes.** New
  modules, module retirements, and material updates to
  existing modules require Board approval at Tier B.
- **Analyst tiering changes.** Movement of an analyst between
  Tiers 1, 2, and 3 requires Board approval; entry to and
  exit from the analyst list requires Board approval.
- **Paid-research authorisation.** Batch authorisation
  quarterly, per the paid-research template
  (`../program/...` Appendix D).
- **Above-threshold community and open-source contributions
  to projects not on the approved-project list.** Requires
  argued case (Appendix B) at Tier B or C.
- **Content submitter behaviour changes.** Where a submitter
  repeatedly submits content mis-classified against tiers,
  the Board may direct education, escalate to the submitter's
  line manager, or (rare) suspend the submitter's programme-
  surface publication privileges pending remediation.

### 3.2 Advisory positions

The EVRB is advisory (not binding) on:

- **Individual technical content within a Distinguished
  voice's own domain.** The Board reviews for surface-
  appropriate content, IP posture, confidentiality, and
  thesis alignment; it does not overrule the Distinguished
  voice on the technical substance itself. Overruling on
  substance is a Chief AI Scientist / CTO-line escalation.
- **Personal-channel publication by employees.** Personal
  channels are covered by the Employee External
  Communications Conduct Policy, adjudicated by HR / Legal /
  CISO. The Board is informed of conduct-policy incidents
  that touched a programme surface (e.g., personal-channel
  content that later moved to a programme surface via
  re-posting) and adjusts guardrails.
- **Press-crisis response tactics.** The Board's role in
  crisis response is to make Tier D approvals rapidly and
  to preserve archival records; Communications leads the
  external delivery under the Chief Communications Officer's
  own escalation authority.
- **Recruiting-marketing decisions.** HR owns those; the
  Board's programme output may inform them but does not
  govern them.

### 3.3 Explicitly out of scope

The EVRB does not adjudicate:

- Halcyon's internal all-hands, employee-forum, or town-hall
  content. Internal Communications owns those.
- Sales collateral used only inside customer meetings under
  NDA (though the EBC curriculum is in scope).
- Investor-facing filings, earnings-call scripts, and
  investor-day content. Investor Relations owns those under
  the CFO's authority; the Board provides the technical
  content and reviewed narrative.
- Halcyon's Responsible AI framework itself (Project 403);
  the Board owns publication surfaces for that content, not
  the framework.

## 4. Quorum and decision rules

### 4.1 Quorum

- **Tier B decisions.** Quorum is the four required sign-off
  seats (Communications, Legal, Security, relevant technical
  voice). Where the relevant technical voice cannot attend, a
  named substitute at Distinguished level may serve.
- **Tier C decisions.** Quorum is five of the fixed-role
  voting seats. Chair, Legal, and one of Security or Product
  are non-optional.
- **Tier D decisions.** Quorum is one on-call reviewer plus
  General Counsel (or explicit delegate) plus Communications
  lead. Full-Board ratification within five business days.

### 4.2 Decision rule

- Consensus preferred. Where consensus is not reached, the
  Board decides by majority of voting seats present. Dissent
  is recorded.
- The Chair does not have a casting vote. On a tied vote at
  Tier C, the decision defers to the next Board meeting or
  escalates to the Board Strategy Committee liaison at the
  submitter's request.
- Silent approval is not permitted. A seat that abstains
  does not count as a positive vote. A seat that does not
  attend records absence.

### 4.3 Dissent recording

Dissent is a first-class artifact. Dissent records:

- Which seat dissented.
- The dissenting position (one to three sentences, contributed
  by the dissenter).
- Whether the dissent is: procedural (workflow issue),
  substantive (position issue), or risk (unaddressed exposure).
- The Board's response — did the dissent change the decision,
  the conditions, or nothing.

Dissent above a threshold (three or more dissenting seats on a
Tier C decision, or two on a decision touching regulatory
consultation or SOX-adjacent investor content) triggers
escalation to the Board Strategy Committee liaison for
awareness. The Board Strategy Committee may direct the Board
to re-review.

## 5. Cadence

### 5.1 Standing meetings

- **Weekly Tier B / Tier C queue review.** Standing 90 minutes.
  Attendees: chair, Legal, Security, Communications operator,
  and the technical / product voices whose queue items are on
  the agenda. Async approvals for straightforward Tier B items
  between meetings.
- **Monthly portfolio review.** Standing 120 minutes.
  Attendees: full Board (fixed-role seats). Reviews the metrics
  scorecard, the review-workflow adherence data, the incident
  log, and any pending Tier C decisions.
- **Quarterly Board Strategy Committee review.** Standing 60
  minutes with the Board Strategy Committee liaison. Reports
  headline metrics, dissent above threshold, standards-body
  entry/exit decisions of the period, and any thesis-alignment
  concerns.
- **Annual thesis review.** Standing offsite. Reviews the
  thesis against the year's outcomes, considers thesis updates,
  and re-approves the surface map, audience map, and voice
  catalogue.

### 5.2 Emergency convenement

- Tier D reviews convene on the on-call rota within the same
  business day. Rota published quarterly with named on-call
  reviewers.
- The Chair may convene a full-Board emergency session for
  significant incidents; Board Strategy Committee liaison is
  notified.

### 5.3 Async and remote

- Async approval is permitted for Tier A and straightforward
  Tier B items where reviewers can inspect the artifact and
  record sign-off within the tier's target latency.
- Remote attendance is standard. In-person convenement is
  reserved for annual thesis review and specific decisions the
  Chair judges require it.

## 6. Standing agenda

Every weekly meeting runs the standing agenda:

1. Confirmation of prior week's decisions (archive check).
2. Tier D incidents in the period; post-incident review.
3. Tier C queue: convened decisions.
4. Tier B queue: approvals, conditions, deferrals.
5. Standing-invitee updates: Analyst Relations, Executive
   Briefing Centre, OSPO, Regulatory Affairs (as applicable).
6. Confidential-material submissions requiring escalation.
7. Cross-programme interactions: Project 403 (Responsible AI
   narrative), Project 404 (Innovation Programme publication
   surfaces).
8. Any other business.

Every monthly meeting adds:

- Metrics-scorecard review.
- Review-workflow adherence data.
- Voice-catalogue distribution review.
- Incident log review.
- Standards-body register review.

Every quarterly meeting with Board Strategy Committee liaison
adds:

- Board scorecard.
- Dissent-above-threshold reporting.
- Thesis-alignment concerns.
- Deferred items requiring escalation.

Every annual thesis review adds:

- Thesis re-approval or amendment.
- Surface map, audience map, voice catalogue re-approval.
- External programme review results.
- Roadmap for the next annual cycle.

## 7. Conflicts of interest

### 7.1 Standing declarations

Fixed-role seats maintain standing declarations of interest:

- External board memberships and advisory roles.
- Personal ownership stakes in entities in Halcyon's
  competitive landscape.
- Family or personal relationships with employees of
  Halcyon customers, analysts, competitors, or partner
  organisations that could reasonably create a conflict.

Declarations are refreshed annually and immediately upon a
material change.

### 7.2 Per-decision conflicts

Where a specific decision touches a declared interest, the
conflicted seat:

- Discloses the conflict at the top of the decision item.
- Withdraws from voting on that item.
- May contribute factual information (with the Chair's
  agreement) but does not participate in the argued
  deliberation.

The Chair may recuse a seat where a conflict is disclosed
mid-discussion.

### 7.3 Programme-adjacent conflicts

- Fixed-role seats do not personally hold analyst-firm
  paid engagements (analyst-firm advisory boards,
  compensated speaking to analyst-firm audiences).
- Fixed-role seats do not personally hold competing
  external thought-leadership programme engagements at
  peer organisations.
- Fixed-role seats disclose participation in industry
  consortia and community-project stewardship roles
  covered by the voice catalogue; these are typical, not
  disqualifying, but must be visible to the Chair.

## 8. Escalation

### 8.1 Upward escalation

The following items escalate from the EVRB to the Board
Strategy Committee liaison:

- Tier C decisions with dissent above threshold (§4.3).
- Standards-body entry/exit decisions with a cost-of-
  compliance impact above a stated materiality threshold
  (approved annually with the CFO).
- Regulatory-consultation content whose implications extend
  beyond programme-surface publication (e.g., Halcyon
  commits to a position at a regulator that has group-wide
  operating implications).
- Incidents rated High or Critical severity per
  Communications' incident-severity matrix.
- Programme-metric misses on two consecutive quarters.

### 8.2 Downward direction

The Board Strategy Committee may direct the EVRB to:

- Re-review a decision.
- Change a standing policy (thesis, surface map, tiering,
  metrics definitions).
- Reconstitute a fixed-role seat.
- Convene an external programme review outside the annual
  cadence.

Board Strategy Committee directions are recorded in the EVRB
archive and reflected in the operating documents in the next
cycle.

### 8.3 Lateral coordination

The EVRB coordinates without escalation with:

- **Innovation Portfolio Board (Project 404).** Interface
  covers publication surfaces for R&D output, IP posture
  interactions, and standards-body positions with IP
  implications.
- **Responsible AI Board (Project 403).** Interface covers
  publication of Responsible AI content; the EVRB approves
  the publication surface; the RAI Board owns the content.
- **Investor Relations.** Interface covers AI content
  appearing in investor communications.
- **HR / People organisation.** Interface covers voice-
  catalogue changes and conduct-policy incidents that
  touched programme surfaces.

## 9. Records and archive

### 9.1 What is archived

The EVRB archives:

- All Tier B, C, and D decisions with reviewer identities,
  comments, and conditions.
- All approved artifacts (final approved version).
- All standards-body membership decisions.
- All analyst-tiering decisions.
- All paid-research authorisations.
- All Executive Briefing Centre curriculum changes.
- All above-threshold community-contribution approvals.
- Meeting minutes for all standing meetings.
- Dissent records with dissenter identity and position.
- Incident records with root-cause analysis.

### 9.2 Retention

- Standard retention: seven years from decision date.
- Extended retention: standards-body decisions and paid-
  research authorisations retained for the duration of the
  underlying standard's active-and-maintenance period plus
  seven years.
- Regulatory-consultation decisions retained per applicable
  regulatory recordkeeping requirement (typically ten years).

### 9.3 Accessibility

- Board members and standing invitees have full read access.
- Content submitters have read access to their own
  submissions.
- Internal Audit and external audit have read access on
  request.
- Access is logged.

### 9.4 Chain of custody

- The Communications operator is the archive owner.
- The archive is stored in a controlled document management
  system with immutable versioning.
- Decisions may be amended only by explicit follow-on
  decisions; the audit trail is preserved.

---

## 10. Amendment

- This charter is amended by the Board Strategy Committee on
  recommendation from the EVRB Chair.
- Substantive amendments (scope of authority, cadence,
  fixed-role seats, quorum) require Board Strategy Committee
  ratification.
- Operational amendments (agenda ordering, standing-invitee
  additions, non-substantive cadence adjustments) may be
  approved by the EVRB directly, recorded in the next
  quarterly review with the Board Strategy Committee
  liaison.
- The charter is reviewed at the annual thesis review at
  minimum.
