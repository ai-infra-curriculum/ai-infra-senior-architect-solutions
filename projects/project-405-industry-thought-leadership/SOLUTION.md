# SOLUTION — Industry Thought Leadership Programme

> Read this *after* you have drafted your own thought-leadership
> programme. This is the reasoning the reference design is
> defending, not a scoring key. A different programme that
> defends the same properties is a valid answer.

## 1. Solution overview

A senior architect answers four questions in a thought-leadership
programme design review:

1. **What conversations is the programme trying to influence,
   and which is it deliberately not?** — the thesis question.
   Programmes that skip this drift into an events calendar
   ordered by whichever conference had the earliest CFP
   deadline.
2. **How is external voice governed without silencing the
   engineers whose reputation the programme exists to
   amplify?** — the review question. Every failure mode of an
   enterprise thought-leadership programme lives on the
   spectrum between "publish anything, apologise later" and
   "route everything through Communications, kill the technical
   voice." The reference programme picks a defensible point on
   that spectrum and defends it.
3. **Where does the programme spend its scarce assets — named
   spokespeople, standards-body seats, executive-briefing time,
   community-contribution time?** — the portfolio question.
   Programmes that answer "everywhere" have no portfolio.
4. **How does the programme measure itself against influence,
   not activity?** — the metrics question. Impressions,
   followers, keynote counts, and press mentions are trivially
   gameable. A senior programme instruments itself against the
   outcomes it is actually funded to produce.

The reference target state is a **thesis-anchored external-voice
programme** governed by an External Voice Review Board with
scoped decision rights, operating a small set of named surfaces
(standards, analyst relations, executive briefings, community
contribution, technical publication, and executive communications),
each with a written participation model and a review workflow
proportional to its risk.

The **key trade-off accepted** is between speed and safety: the
review workflow adds calendar time between "engineer has an
insight" and "insight is public." The reference programme accepts
that cost and defends it, because the alternative — no review,
faster publication, higher tail risk — is not survivable at
Halcyon's scale. The workflow is designed to add days to routine
content and hours to standards-position revisions, not weeks to
either.

## 2. Worked answer (the reference programme)

### 2.1 The programme thesis (three sentences, then arguments)

The reference programme opens with a written thesis at three
sentences, no more:

> *Halcyon's external voice on AI exists to make the standards,
> analyst categorisations, and hiring pipelines that will shape
> industrial-AI competition over the next decade line up with
> Halcyon's engineering positions. The programme spends its
> scarce assets — named spokespeople, standards seats, and
> executive-briefing time — where Halcyon has substantive
> engineering positions that other actors do not, and stays
> silent where it does not. The programme's audience is
> technical decision-makers at Halcyon's customers, standards
> participants who influence Halcyon's cost of compliance, and
> senior applied-AI engineers Halcyon needs to hire — not the
> general public and not the consumer-AI press.*

That thesis does three things a communications strategy cannot:

- It states which conversations the programme is contesting
  (industrial-AI standards, industrial-AI analyst categorisation,
  senior applied-AI hiring) and by exclusion which it is not
  chasing (general AI ethics discourse, consumer-AI news cycles,
  cross-industry AGI debate).
- It names the audiences (technical decision-makers at
  customers, standards participants, senior engineers) and
  excludes others (general public, retail investors, consumer-
  AI press). Programmes that try to reach everyone reach no one.
- It gives the External Voice Review Board a criterion against
  which to test proposed activity: does this artifact influence
  one of the three named conversations for one of the three
  named audiences? If not, the artifact is off-thesis; it may
  still be published but not on programme budget.

**Why a written thesis and not a mission statement.** A mission
statement accumulates consensus adjectives ("thought-leading,
responsible, innovative, trusted"). A thesis is falsifiable: a
Board member can argue with it, and the argument is where the
programme's spending decisions get made. If the Board wants to
change the programme, the thesis is what changes; the machinery
does not.

### 2.2 The surfaces map — enumerating where Halcyon's voice appears

The reference programme enumerates the surfaces on which Halcyon
speaks about AI. Enumeration matters: if the programme cannot
name the surfaces, it cannot govern them.

The reference surfaces map has six categories:

- **Standards and regulatory bodies.** Working-group
  participation, position papers, public consultation
  responses. Highest-consequence surface: a position adopted
  into an ISO/IEC standard binds Halcyon and its competitors
  for years.
- **Analyst relations.** Analyst briefings, analyst inquiries,
  paid research, analyst-report review windows. High-
  consequence, lower-latency than standards.
- **Executive briefings and customer-facing technical
  presentations.** The Executive Briefing Centre; customer
  advisory boards; user-conference keynotes on Halcyon
  platforms. Direct-revenue-adjacent.
- **Technical publications and conference presentations.**
  Refereed publications, industrial-track workshop papers, and
  invited keynotes at third-party technical conferences.
  Reputational and hiring-signal-heavy.
- **Community and open-source contribution.** Contributions to
  open-source projects, community consortia, safety-benchmark
  efforts, and public evaluation harnesses. Long-arc,
  strategically valuable, high governance need because of IP
  and licensing exposure.
- **Executive communications and press.** CEO / CTO / CPO
  external interviews, keynote presentations to investor
  audiences, and press statements about AI. Board- and
  Investor-Relations-adjacent surface, governed accordingly.

**What is deliberately not on the map.** Individual-contributor
personal social-media presence is not a programme surface even
when the engineer works at Halcyon. The programme's policy on
personal channels is a *conduct* policy (do not disclose
Halcyon-confidential material, do not represent yourself as
speaking for Halcyon when you are not), not a review workflow.
Trying to govern personal channels as programme surfaces was
tried elsewhere in the industry and produced two failure modes
in parallel: engineers stopped posting entirely, and engineers
who kept posting went off-Halcyon-brand faster because they
resented the workflow. The reference programme accepts this
trade.

### 2.3 The voice catalogue — who at Halcyon speaks, in what capacity

The reference programme enumerates a **voice catalogue** — the set
of Halcyon roles authorised to speak on AI on programme
surfaces, and the surfaces each is authorised for.

- **Executive voices** (CEO, CTO, CPO, CFO on AI-financial
  questions, CISO on AI-security questions). Authorised for
  executive communications, press, executive briefings, and
  by invitation for keynote surfaces. Content prepared with
  the External Voice Review Board and Communications; delivery
  by the executive.
- **Principal / Distinguished technical voices** (Chief
  Scientist, Distinguished Engineers, Principal Researchers).
  Authorised for standards, technical publication, keynote
  surfaces, and community contribution above threshold.
  Individual named delegates for standards seats.
- **Engineering-manager and staff-engineer voices.** Authorised
  for technical publication, community contribution below
  threshold, and specific analyst briefings by nomination.
  Standards participation as working-group members supporting
  a Principal delegate.
- **Individual-contributor engineer voices.** Authorised for
  technical publication co-authorship, community contribution
  in defined categories, and technical-conference presentation
  by nomination. Personal social-media presence per the
  conduct policy, not a programme surface.
- **Communications / analyst-relations professional voices.**
  Authorised for analyst-relations coordination, press
  coordination, and executive-communications preparation.
  Deliver the machinery; do not deliver technical positions
  as the technical voice.

**Why a catalogue and not "anyone with something to say".** A
catalogue lets the programme allocate scarce spokesperson time
to conversations that matter, allocate scarce technical
preparation time to spokespeople who need it, and audit which
voices have appeared on which surfaces over time. It does not
lock other engineers out of publishing: a staff engineer who
wants to present at a technical conference is nominated,
prepared, and delivered by the programme; the programme's
answer is expected to be "yes" more often than "no" for
technical-conference surfaces because those are exactly the
hiring-signal surfaces the thesis names.

### 2.4 The review workflow — the risk-tiered review path

The reference programme runs a **risk-tiered review workflow**.
The tier is set by the surface and the content class, not by
who wants to publish. The tiers correspond to review latency
Halcyon can absorb.

**Tier A — Standing review** (target latency: 24–72 hours).

- Technical-conference talks reusing previously-reviewed
  material.
- Community-contribution pull requests below threshold to
  approved projects.
- Analyst briefing content reusing previously-briefed material.
- Reviewer: designated technical lead in the External Voice
  Review Board rotation, cross-check by Communications
  designee.

**Tier B — Substantive review** (target latency: 3–10 business
days).

- New technical publications and conference talks.
- New analyst-briefing narratives.
- Community-contribution pull requests above threshold.
- Standards-body position revisions (unless emergency).
- New executive-briefing curriculum modules.
- Reviewers: External Voice Review Board members from
  Communications, Legal, Security, and the relevant Halcyon
  business unit. IP designee consulted for community/open-
  source items.

**Tier C — Full board review** (target latency: 5–15 business
days).

- New standards-body position papers.
- New executive-communications strategic messaging.
- Public consultation responses to regulators.
- Any content involving unreleased-product roadmaps or
  customer-attributable information.
- Any content revising Halcyon's public Responsible AI posture.
- Reviewers: External Voice Review Board convenes; Legal and
  Security are non-optional; Board Strategy Committee liaison
  informed.

**Tier D — Emergency review** (target latency: same-business-day
where possible).

- Response to a mis-representation in a published analyst
  report, standards-body public draft, or press item.
- Response to an incident-adjacent question from a regulator
  or a major customer.
- Reviewers: External Voice Review Board on-call rota;
  General Counsel available; Communications leads external
  delivery.

**Why tiered and not uniform.** A single review tier is either
too slow for routine work or too permissive for standards
positions. The reference programme accepts that the review
overhead is not free and controls the overhead by tiering
proportional to risk.

**Defaults on ambiguity.** Where the tier is genuinely unclear,
the artifact is reviewed at the higher tier. Explicit rule:
the burden of proof for Tier A treatment sits with the requester;
the burden of proof for Tier C escalation does not.

**What the workflow produces.** Every reviewed artifact enters
the archive with: the tier at which it was reviewed, the named
reviewers, the resolved comments and their disposition, the
final approved version, and any conditions attached (embargo
window, embargo release date, one-off vs. reusable).

### 2.5 Standards-participation model — participation by argument

The reference programme's standards-participation model has
three commitments.

**Commitment 1 — Membership is argued and time-boxed.** Every
standards-body membership is on file with a written argument
addressing:

- **What is Halcyon's substantive engineering position in this
  body?** Not the marketing intent — the technical position
  Halcyon can defend under cross-examination.
- **What is the cost-of-compliance impact if the standard is
  written against Halcyon's constraints?** Named product
  lines, named cost drivers, ordered by magnitude.
- **Who is the named delegate, at what role level, with what
  travel and preparation budget?** Delegate rotation policy
  and successor named ex ante.
- **What is the exit?** A body Halcyon is no longer a
  member of has a stated exit reason, exit date, and a
  handover of any active positions.

The reference programme's active-membership shortlist for a
Halcyon-shaped organisation:

- **ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42** (Artificial Intelligence). Primary
  standards vehicle for 42001, 23894, 42005 and successors.
  Non-negotiable membership.
- **IEC TC 65 / SC 65A and equivalents** for industrial-
  control safety and functional safety. Halcyon's safety-
  critical control loops sit here; Halcyon has substantive
  engineering positions on the intersection with AI
  components. Non-negotiable.
- **IEC TC 65 / SC 65E** and adjacent for industrial
  cybersecurity (IEC 62443 family). Cyber-physical AI
  systems overlap.
- **ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27** (information security /
  cybersecurity) selectively, where AI system security
  overlaps the Halcyon product surface.
- **IEEE Standards Association** working groups on ethically-
  aligned design and on specific AI safety and audit
  standards where Halcyon has a substantive engineering
  position; membership by working group, not blanket.

**Committed non-participation** (with argued reasons):

- Consumer-facing AI content-moderation standards. Not
  Halcyon's market; not Halcyon's engineering.
- General-purpose AI governance forums whose output is
  political declarations rather than technical standards.
- Marketing-driven industry consortia without a working-
  group deliverable programme.

**Commitment 2 — Positions are prepared and archived.** Every
position Halcyon takes into a working-group meeting is
prepared by the named delegate in written form, reviewed at
Tier B or Tier C depending on standards impact, and archived
with meeting date and delegate. Positions are not created in
the room; positions are refined in the room from a prepared
draft.

**Commitment 3 — IP posture is defined ex ante.** Halcyon's
standards-essential-patent posture (RAND / FRAND terms,
declared essential patents, non-assertion covenants) is
decided per body against the Project 404 IP strategy and is
not renegotiated meeting-by-meeting. Delegates carry the IP
posture into the room in writing.

**Why argued, time-boxed, and archived.** Standards
participation is high-consequence and slow-feedback. A
programme that joins bodies out of inertia and cannot say why
it is a member accumulates seats it does not staff and cannot
argue for at CFO review. The reference programme trades
breadth for defensibility.

### 2.6 Analyst-relations model — briefing cadence, argued research

The reference analyst-relations model has three commitments.

**Commitment 1 — Analysts are tiered and briefed on a cadence.**
The reference programme maintains a named analyst list
covering the industrial-AI, industrial-automation, and
enterprise-AI infrastructure markets Halcyon competes in.
Analysts are tiered:

- **Tier 1 — quarterly briefing cadence.** Analysts whose
  reports Halcyon's buyers directly consume when they
  categorise vendors (typical shortlist: two to five
  analysts covering the industrial-automation-plus-AI
  intersection at the tier-one firms).
- **Tier 2 — semi-annual briefing cadence.** Analysts whose
  coverage is adjacent and whose narrative shapes buyer
  language over a longer arc.
- **Tier 3 — annual briefing cadence.** Analysts whose
  coverage is peripheral but occasionally cross-references
  Halcyon.

Analysts outside the list are handled by inbound response,
not by outbound briefing.

**Commitment 2 — The content pipeline into briefings is
Halcyon-owned.** Each briefing runs against a prepared
narrative Halcyon has reviewed at Tier B or C depending on
content. The briefing is Halcyon's story told to the analyst,
not the analyst's questions answered ad-hoc. The prepared
narrative is reused across analysts in a briefing cycle to
prevent inconsistent messaging.

**Commitment 3 — Paid research is bounded, argued, and
governed.** The reference programme distinguishes:

- **Briefings** (analyst inquiries not paid for by Halcyon,
  no compensation, no editorial control by Halcyon on the
  resulting report). Standard operating mode.
- **Paid research** (Halcyon commissions or co-sponsors an
  analyst research artifact, with published disclosure).
  Reserved for arguments that justify the cost against a
  named audience decision. Approved batch quarterly by the
  External Voice Review Board and the Chief Marketing
  Officer.
- **Speaking / consulting engagements** (analyst appears at
  a Halcyon event; standard commercial terms; disclosed
  when required by the analyst firm's ethics policy).

Halcyon does not blur these categories in delivery or in
attribution.

**Why cadence and not opportunistic.** Analysts consume
information from many vendors continuously. Halcyon's
influence on the analyst's mental model is proportional to
the consistency of Halcyon's briefings, not to the impressive-
ness of any single briefing. The reference programme accepts
the discipline.

### 2.7 Executive-briefing model — curriculum, not calendar

The reference programme's Executive Briefing Centre model has
three commitments.

**Commitment 1 — The curriculum is published.** The Executive
Briefing Centre operates against a written curriculum of
briefing modules — the AI-in-industrial-automation narrative,
the specific Halcyon technology narrative, the Responsible AI
posture briefing, the customer-adoption-journey briefing, the
platform-and-partnership briefing. Each module has an owner,
a review cadence, and a target duration.

**Commitment 2 — Intake is criterion-based.** Not every
customer meeting merits an Executive Briefing Centre engagement.
The intake criteria are: customer strategic importance, AI
adoption horizon in the customer's roadmap, and the specific
question the customer is asking. Sales requests briefings;
programme accepts or declines against criteria; declined
requests receive a lighter-weight artifact (a briefing
handout, a webinar recording) rather than a bespoke session.

**Commitment 3 — Post-briefing artifact hand-off is defined.**
Every briefing ends with a documented set of artifacts handed
to sales for follow-up. The programme's obligation ends at
handoff; the sales conversation is not the programme's
responsibility. The programme's own metric on the briefing is
the customer's *return* to the Executive Briefing Centre or
progression on a named platform-adoption path, not the sales
outcome per se.

**Executive sponsor rotation.** The Executive Briefing Centre
runs against a rotating executive-sponsor model: no single
Halcyon executive is the "AI face" of the Executive Briefing
Centre. Sponsors rotate on an eighteen-month cadence. The
reason is exactly the reason the Portfolio Board in Project
404 has scoped external members: a programme whose external
face is one person becomes that person's programme and does
not survive their departure.

### 2.8 Community and open-source model — argued contributions

The reference community-and-open-source model has four
commitments.

**Commitment 1 — Contribution categories are enumerated.**
Halcyon's contributions to open-source AI infrastructure fall
into four categories:

- **Infrastructure category** (contributions to widely-used
  ML frameworks, inference runtimes, compiler toolchains,
  and evaluation harnesses that raise the industry floor
  without giving away Halcyon differentiation).
- **Benchmark and evaluation category** (contributions to
  public benchmarks, safety-critical evaluation suites, and
  domain-specific evaluation harnesses that shape how AI
  systems in industrial-automation are measured).
- **Standards-reference-implementation category** (open-
  source reference implementations of standards Halcyon
  participates in, released to accelerate adoption of the
  standard).
- **Community-good category** (contributions to tooling and
  documentation that support the broader engineering
  community; low strategic value directly, but positive
  hiring-signal externality).

Contributions in each category run to different review
depths.

**Commitment 2 — Contributions above threshold have written
arguments.** Every above-threshold contribution has a written
strategic argument on file addressing:

- **Which contribution category does this fit, and why?**
- **What is the strategic effect on Halcyon?** Directly or
  as externality.
- **What is the licence exposure and the IP interaction with
  the Project 404 IP strategy?** Halcyon does not open-source
  product-differentiating capability; contributions are
  audited against that rule.
- **Who is the named maintainer commitment?** A contribution
  that Halcyon merges once and abandons is worse than not
  contributing: the community reads it as a signal of intent
  Halcyon then fails to deliver.

**Commitment 3 — Community-steward roles are named.** Where a
Halcyon employee serves as a maintainer, technical steering
committee member, or programme committee member for an
external community project, the role is named in the voice
catalogue and the time commitment is on record. Employees
serving as community stewards on Halcyon time do so on
approved projects; the review workflow for their contributions
runs at Tier A (routine) or Tier B (novel).

**Commitment 4 — Personal contributions on personal time are
covered by the conduct policy, not the review workflow.**
Consistent with section 2.2, this is a conduct policy: Halcyon
IP does not leave, Halcyon customer information does not leave,
Halcyon-attributable positions on unreleased products do not
leave. Otherwise engineers contribute to the community on their
own terms.

**Why enumerated categories.** Every open-source programme that
failed at scale failed because it lost the ability to say why
it was contributing. A contribution portfolio without categories
is a spending line the CFO cannot defend and a legal exposure
the General Counsel cannot bound.

### 2.9 Metrics — a small scorecard, gaming-resistant by design

The reference programme instruments itself against a scorecard
of five headline metrics and a small set of hygiene metrics.
As in Project 404, the scorecard is deliberately small; a
fifteen-metric scorecard optimises for the average of things
nobody cares about most.

**Headline metrics (reported to the Board Strategy Committee
quarterly):**

1. **Standards positions adopted.** Named positions Halcyon
   contributed that were adopted (in whole or in
   substantive part) into standards drafts or into final
   published standards in the period. The metric that most
   directly measures the "we shape the standards" claim in
   the thesis.
2. **Analyst-report placement outcomes.** Movement in named
   analyst-report positioning for Halcyon in the period —
   entering coverage, upward movement in a quadrant or
   wave-diagram equivalent, sustained mention in
   competitive-shortlists. Analyst-quoted Halcyon language
   is a supplementary hygiene indicator, not a headline.
3. **Hiring signal attributable to programme output.**
   Applicants and offers-accepted where the candidate cites a
   Halcyon publication, conference presentation, open-source
   contribution, or public technical talk as their reason
   for applying, in structured intake data. Measured by HR
   attribution, not by programme self-report.
4. **Executive briefing conversion.** Customers who
   completed an Executive Briefing Centre engagement in the
   period and progressed on a named platform-adoption path
   within the following two quarters. Measured by sales-
   operations attribution, not by Executive Briefing Centre
   self-report.
5. **Review-workflow adherence.** Proportion of external
   artifacts published in the period that went through the
   correct review tier, with the required reviewer sign-offs
   on file. This is the "no drift" metric; a programme that
   is winning on 1–4 but losing on 5 is one incident away
   from a Board-level problem.

**Hygiene metrics (reported semi-annually or on request):**

- Content-pipeline volume by surface (standards positions
  drafted, analyst briefings delivered, technical
  publications submitted, community contributions merged,
  executive briefings delivered).
- Review-workflow cycle time by tier, with distribution.
- Voice catalogue distribution — which named voices appeared
  on which surfaces, to guard against spokesperson over-
  concentration.
- Incident count — external artifacts that required
  correction, retraction, or embargo revision post-
  publication, with root cause.

**Metrics deliberately not counted at the headline:**

- Impressions, followers, social-media reach, viral video
  counts. Trivially gameable and, worse, deceptive: high
  reach on off-thesis content is a net negative for the
  programme, not a positive.
- Number of keynotes delivered, number of conferences
  attended. Activity metrics; do not distinguish a keynote
  at a body Halcyon is trying to influence from a keynote
  at a body Halcyon is not.
- Number of open-source pull requests merged. Trivially
  gameable; the strategic categorisation matters, not the
  count.
- Number of press mentions. Includes negative mentions,
  irrelevant mentions, and mis-attributed mentions;
  aggregating them treats a defensive-crisis quote as
  equivalent to a thesis-adjacent thought-piece.

**How the metrics are protected against Goodhart drift.**

- The headline metrics are all measured *outside* the
  programme (HR attribution, sales-operations attribution,
  standards-body records, analyst-firm reports). The
  programme does not score itself.
- Review-workflow adherence is measured against the archive,
  not against the reviewer's memory. A missing signature
  in the archive counts as non-adherence regardless of
  whether the review "happened".
- The Board Strategy Committee reviews the scorecard
  definition annually and reserves the right to change it.
  The definition change itself is a governance artifact.

### 2.10 Governance — the External Voice Review Board

The External Voice Review Board is chartered in
`governance/external-voice-charter.md`. Key properties:

- **Composition.** Fixed-role, including: Chief Marketing
  Officer or delegate (chair), General Counsel or delegate,
  Chief Information Security Officer or delegate, AI Lab
  director, Chief Product Officer or delegate, at least one
  Distinguished / Principal engineering voice, and a
  standing Communications operator (executes the workflow;
  does not vote on technical positions). Board Strategy
  Committee liaison attends by invitation for Tier C
  decisions.
- **Authority.** Binding on: review-tier assignment, Tier B
  and Tier C approvals, standards-body membership entry and
  exit, executive-briefing curriculum sign-off, community-
  and-open-source contribution above threshold, paid-
  research authorisation. Advisory on: individual voice
  performance (which is HR / manager scope), press-crisis
  response (Communications scope, informed by review-board
  context), and specific technical content (Distinguished
  engineering voices own the technical position).
- **Cadence.** Weekly standing meeting for Tier B / Tier C
  approvals; monthly portfolio review; quarterly review with
  the Board Strategy Committee; annual thesis review.
- **Emergency convenement.** Tier D reviews convene on the
  on-call rota within the same business day.
- **Dissent recording.** Approvals record dissenting
  positions explicitly. Dissent above a threshold triggers
  escalation to the Board Strategy Committee liaison.

**Why Review Board and not "editorial committee".** Editorial
committees advise on tone. The External Voice Review Board is
authoritative on publication decisions on programme surfaces;
that authority is what keeps the programme from drifting into
"the Communications team publishes whatever the loudest
engineer wrote."

### 2.11 Roadmap — twenty-four months, sequenced by reversibility

**Phase 0 (Months 0–3) — Thesis, surfaces, voice catalogue.**
Programme thesis drafted, reviewed with the Board Strategy
Committee, ratified. Surfaces enumerated; existing standards
memberships and analyst relationships inventoried against the
enumeration. Voice catalogue drafted with HR and named
Distinguished / Principal engineers. External Voice Review
Board chartered and seated. **Highly reversible.** No workflow
changes yet; existing external activity continues under
existing arrangements.

**Phase 1 (Months 3–9) — Review workflow live; standards
positions on programme cadence.** Review workflow published
and mandatory for all new content on programme surfaces.
Standards memberships reviewed against the argued-list; two
to three exits published where the current membership does
not meet the criteria. Analyst tiering completed; briefing
cadence for Tier 1 analysts running to schedule. Executive
Briefing Centre curriculum drafted; first three modules
approved and delivered. **Medium reversibility.**

**Phase 2 (Months 9–18) — Backfill and steady state.** All
existing standards positions carried onto the programme
cadence. Community and open-source contribution registry
authoritative. Analyst-report placement outcomes measured for
the first annual cycle. Voice catalogue refreshed; second
executive-sponsor for the Executive Briefing Centre named
against the rotation model. Metrics scorecard reported
quarterly. **Low reversibility per membership exit** — leaving
a standards body is expensive in relationships — but the
reference programme front-loads the honest exits rather than
sustaining them.

**Phase 3 (Months 18–24) — External review and thesis
refresh.** First external programme review, informed by
comparators (industry-peer disclosure of standards
participation, analyst-firm methodology reviews, community-
contribution portfolios). Thesis reviewed against results;
Board Strategy Committee approves any thesis update.

**Beyond month 24.** The programme runs on an annual thesis
review, semi-annual review-workflow audit, and continuous
tiered review. There is no "phase 4"; the programme is now
steady state, and steady state is the point.

**Why sequenced by reversibility.** Same reasoning as Projects
402, 403, 404. Chartering a Review Board is cheap and
reversible; exiting a standards body Halcyon is committed to
is not. The reference sequences the cheap decisions early so
the expensive ones can be defended.

### 2.12 What is deferred, and why

Some items are deliberately deferred beyond the reference:

- **Specific analyst-firm master service agreements for paid
  research and speaking engagements.** The programme's
  posture is defined; the specific commercial terms with
  named firms are a Procurement / CMO decision informed by
  Phase 1. Tracked as **FOLLOWUP-ANALYST-01**.
- **Regional adaptations for Japan, South Korea, and mainland
  China.** Halcyon operates in markets whose standards
  participation norms, analyst ecosystems, and community-
  contribution posture differ materially. The reference
  programme is the group frame; regional adaptations are
  designed in Phase 1 with regional leadership. Tracked as
  **FOLLOWUP-REGION-01**.
- **Interaction with Halcyon's Investor Relations posture on
  AI.** Investor communications about AI (in filings,
  earnings calls, investor days) sit under Investor
  Relations and the CFO. The programme provides the
  technical content and the reviewed narrative; the
  delivery is governed by Investor Relations. Tracked as
  **FOLLOWUP-IR-01**.

Deferrals are scoped, time-bound, and tracked. They are not
open research questions blocking the reference programme.

## Implementation

The implementation approach is defined by the roadmap in
Section 2.11, the review workflow in Section 2.4, and the
metrics in Section 2.9. This section names the invariants an
implementation team must hold:

- **Thesis-first.** No programme decision is defensible in the
  absence of the thesis. If the thesis is not written and
  ratified in Phase 0, every downstream argument becomes a
  personality contest between engineering, marketing, and
  legal.
- **Review workflow is the definition of published.** An
  artifact that appears on a programme surface without a
  completed review record is a governance failure, not a
  content success. Fix the process before scaling the volume.
- **Standards membership is argued, not inherited.** Every
  membership on file has a written argument. Every membership
  without an argument is either argued or exited within a
  named timeframe.
- **Analyst briefings run on cadence.** A briefing calendar
  that "slips" quarterly slips into "briefings when there's
  news", which slips into "briefings as damage control".
  The reference programme protects the cadence.
- **Executive Briefing Centre curriculum is published.** A
  curriculum-less Executive Briefing Centre is one executive's
  slide deck delivered by whoever is available. The curriculum
  is what makes the Centre survive rotation.
- **Community contributions above threshold have written
  arguments.** Under-threshold contributions may proceed on
  Tier A; above-threshold contributions may not proceed
  without an argument on file.
- **Metrics read from records outside the programme.** HR
  attribution, sales-operations attribution, standards-body
  records, analyst-firm reports. The programme does not
  score itself.
- **Sponsor-neutral operating cadence.** Review Board cadence,
  standards-body cadence, Executive Briefing Centre rotation,
  and thesis review run on schedule regardless of leadership
  transitions. The design does not depend on the current
  CMO or Chief Scientist being in seat.

Sizing, team topology, and per-artifact build ownership are
captured in `program/thought-leadership-program-design.md`;
that document is the operational companion to this SOLUTION.

## 3. Validation steps

A senior architect validates the programme against the
following. If any check fails, the design is not yet ready for
the Board Strategy Committee.

**Structural checks:**

1. Pick a random standards-body membership on the current
   register. Can you produce the argued reason for membership,
   the named delegate, the delegate's current position on the
   working group's active topics, and the exit criterion? All
   in under fifteen minutes? If not, the standards portfolio
   is a spreadsheet.
2. Pick a random external artifact published in the last ninety
   days on a programme surface. Can you produce the review
   tier, the reviewers, the resolved comments, and the
   published version, from the archive? If any of these can
   be resolved from a slide or memory instead of the archive,
   the workflow is aspirational.
3. Show the last three External Voice Review Board decisions.
   For each, name the vote, the dissent recorded, the
   follow-up committed, and the artifact where the decision
   is stored. If any is irretrievable, the Review Board is a
   meeting, not a governance body.

**Portfolio dry-runs:**

4. The Chief Marketing Officer proposes a new mid-tier
   paid-research engagement with a new analyst firm outside
   the current tiered list. Walk through Review Board
   evaluation, argued case, decision, and archival. At which
   points is the proposal blocked, and by what?
5. The AI Lab director is invited to a new standards body
   Halcyon is not currently a member of. Walk through argued-
   membership evaluation, decision to join or decline,
   delegate nomination, and IP-posture confirmation. What
   is the target-latency for the decision?
6. A Principal Engineer proposes an open-source contribution
   above the review threshold to an ML compiler project
   Halcyon has not previously contributed to. Walk through
   category classification, argued case, IP interaction with
   Project 404, and community-steward-role decision. When is
   the contribution posted?

**Incident dry-runs:**

7. A junior researcher posts a technical detail on a public
   forum that Legal identifies as touching an unreleased-
   product roadmap. What is the response path? Where is the
   incident recorded? What review-workflow change results?
8. A Tier-1 analyst's newest industrial-AI report positions
   Halcyon lower than in the prior year, citing a technical
   claim Halcyon believes to be incorrect. What is the Tier D
   response path? Who prepares the response? What does not
   get said in the response, and why?
9. A named standards delegate leaves Halcyon mid-cycle. What
   breaks? (Correct answer: not the programme; possibly the
   specific working-group presence; the named-successor
   clause in the delegate policy governs the transition.)

If steps 1–9 can be walked through without hand-waving, the
programme is review-ready. If any require "we would need to
build that," the programme is not yet complete.

## 4. Rubric — quick reference

The full rubric lives at `rubric/evaluation-rubric.md`. The six
weighted dimensions are:

1. **Programme thesis defensibility** (weight: 20%). Is there
   a written thesis, and does it exclude conversations and
   audiences as much as it includes them?
2. **Review workflow enforceability** (weight: 20%). Is
   "reviewed at the correct tier with reviewers on file" the
   definition of published, with an archive to prove it?
3. **Standards-participation model** (weight: 15%). Is
   membership argued and time-boxed, with delegates, positions,
   and IP posture on file?
4. **Analyst and executive-briefing model** (weight: 15%). Are
   analysts tiered with a briefing cadence, and does the
   Executive Briefing Centre run against a published
   curriculum with a rotating sponsor?
5. **Metrics resistance to gaming** (weight: 15%). Do the
   headline metrics reward influence outcomes, sourced
   outside the programme, and deliberately not headline
   impressions or activity counts?
6. **External Voice Review Board governance** (weight: 15%).
   Is the Board scoped, binding on the classes named, with
   dissent recorded and an emergency path?

A submission scoring below 60 on Dimension 1 (thesis) or
Dimension 2 (review workflow) is non-passing regardless of
other scores. A senior architect who cannot show either "what
we are trying to influence" or "how content actually gets
reviewed" has not demonstrated the level.

## 5. Common mistakes

**1. A content calendar instead of a programme thesis.** A
calendar says what the programme is *doing*; a thesis says
what the programme is *for*, in a way that can be argued with.
Calendars accumulate; theses decide.

**2. Review workflow that everyone routes around.** A workflow
whose latency is measured in weeks for routine content will
be worked around within one quarter. The reference programme
tiers latency to risk, and defends the tiering so the routine
tier stays fast.

**3. Standards membership by inertia.** Bodies Halcyon joined
five years ago because someone was invited, with no delegate,
no position, and no argued reason. The reference programme
either argues the membership or exits it.

**4. Analyst relations as damage control.** Programmes that
brief analysts only when a report is imminent lose the
narrative arc. The reference programme runs on cadence and
treats a bad report as diagnostic of missed briefings, not as
a briefing trigger.

**5. Executive Briefing Centre as one executive's stage.** If
the Executive Briefing Centre curriculum is one CxO's slide
deck, it does not survive rotation. The reference programme
runs to a published curriculum with rotating sponsors.

**6. Open-source contribution as vanity.** Contributions merged
once and then abandoned damage Halcyon's community reputation
more than not contributing. The reference programme requires
maintainer commitment on above-threshold contributions.

**7. Metrics that measure the programme, not the influence.**
Impressions, follower counts, keynote counts, PR mentions.
The reference scorecard measures outcomes the programme is
funded to produce and lets activity be a hygiene metric.

**8. Personal channels governed as programme surfaces.**
Trying to route engineers' personal social-media presence
through the Review Board creates the two failure modes at
once — engineers stop posting, and the engineers who post
resent the programme. The reference programme uses conduct
policy, not workflow, for personal channels.

**9. Review Board as editorial committee.** A body that
advises but does not decide leaves the loudest voice as the
default. The reference Board is binding on the listed
classes and dissent-recording is a first-class practice.

**10. Named-person dependencies.** If the programme only works
while the current Chief Scientist is doing the standards work,
the programme is a person's programme, not an enterprise
capability. Every role in the reference programme is a role,
not a name; delegate rotation and named-successor clauses are
non-optional.
