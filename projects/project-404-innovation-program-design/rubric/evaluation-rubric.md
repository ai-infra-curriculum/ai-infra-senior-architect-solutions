# Evaluation Rubric — AI Innovation Programme Design

> Grading rubric for learner submissions to Project 404. The
> rubric weights **defensibility of the portfolio thesis** and
> **enforceability of the transfer contract** above **breadth of
> the idea catalogue**. A learner who submits a lab with fifteen
> exciting projects and no transfer contract will score below a
> learner who submits five defended bets and a working transfer
> path.

**Scoring model.** Six weighted dimensions, 0–100 within each,
then weighted-summed to a final 0–100 programme score. Two
dimensions are non-passing thresholds: a submission scoring
below 60 on either Dimension 1 (portfolio thesis) or Dimension 2
(transfer contract) is non-passing regardless of other scores.

**Grader posture.** The rubric is written for a reviewer who
would sit on the Innovation Portfolio Board or the Board
Strategy Committee — someone who is testing whether they could
work from this submission if it were their own programme, not
whether the submission looks impressive.

---

## Dimension 1 — Portfolio thesis defensibility (weight: 20%)

**What the grader is testing.** Is there a written thesis, does
the thesis exclude as much as it includes, and could a Board
Strategy Committee member redirect the programme by changing
the thesis?

### 1.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Thesis is written in three sentences, states a market position, states what is in and out of scope, and is falsifiable. Excludes named alternatives (consumer AI, cloud-only services, general foundation-model pre-training as end-in-itself, general-purpose academic research) with argued reasons. Portfolio decisions can be traced to the thesis. |
| **75–89** | Thesis is written and scoped. Excludes some alternatives but not all obvious ones. Portfolio decisions mostly trace to the thesis; some are unattached. |
| **60–74** | Thesis is present but reads as a mission statement — accumulates consensus adjectives, does not exclude alternatives explicitly. Portfolio decisions are argued but not from the thesis. |
| **40–59** | Thesis is fragmentary or aspirational. Reader cannot tell what the programme is *not* doing. |
| **0–39** | No written thesis, or thesis is a mission statement with no scoping content. |

### 1.2 Grader questions

- Can the thesis be quoted in three sentences?
- Does the thesis name what the programme is not doing, with
  argued reasons?
- If a Board member wanted to redirect the programme, would
  they change the thesis or the project list?
- Are exclusions defended, or hand-waved?

### 1.3 Common down-marks

- Twelve-adjective mission statement (down-mark to at most 60).
- No exclusions named (down-mark to at most 65).
- Thesis exists but portfolio decisions do not reference it
  (down-mark to at most 70).

**Non-passing threshold: below 60 fails this dimension and the
overall submission regardless of other scores.**

## Dimension 2 — Transfer contract enforceability (weight: 20%)

**What the grader is testing.** Is "transferred to a receiving
team" the definition of done for horizon-1 and graduating
horizon-2 projects, and does the submission produce a signed,
auditable artifact as evidence?

### 2.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Transfer contract template exists, is signed at handover, names artifact / characterisation / interface / residual risk / support obligation / transfer test / transfer date. Receiving-team refusal path exists and is recorded. Value estimated by receiving team, not by Lab. Post-transfer scope is defined. |
| **75–89** | Transfer contract exists and covers most elements. Refusal path exists but is under-specified. Value source is receiving team but weakly recorded. |
| **60–74** | Transfer artifact exists but is informal or advisory. Definition-of-done is loose ("handover meeting held" rather than "contract signed"). Refusal path unclear. |
| **40–59** | Transfer is described in prose but there is no artifact-level definition. "Definition of done" is graduation from the Lab, not receiving-team acceptance. |
| **0–39** | No transfer mechanism defined. Projects are done when the Lab says they are done. |

### 2.2 Grader questions

- Can the grader find the transfer contract template?
- Who signs the transfer, and what does refusal look like?
- Where is the residual risk recorded, and who owns it?
- Who estimates the value that lands in the scorecard?

### 2.3 Common down-marks

- No template — only prose (down-mark to at most 55).
- Lab estimates its own value (down-mark to at most 65).
- No refusal path (down-mark to at most 65).
- Support obligation absent or "as needed" (down-mark to at
  most 75).

**Non-passing threshold: below 60 fails this dimension and the
overall submission regardless of other scores.**

---

## Dimension 3 — Emerging-technology evaluation (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Does the programme evaluate
emerging capabilities against a written protocol producing
written verdicts, or against demos and personal enthusiasm?

### 3.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Written evaluation protocol adapted from Heilmeier Catechism or equivalent. Produces one of four categorical verdicts (enter H1 / enter H2 or H3 / park / do not pursue) with archived written argument. Refresh cadence defined for parked entries. Refusal-window defined for do-not-pursue. |
| **75–89** | Written protocol exists; verdicts are categorical. Refresh and refusal-window mostly defined. |
| **60–74** | Protocol described but produces continuous scores or free-form verdicts. Archive practice unclear. |
| **40–59** | Evaluation described as an activity but no protocol. Verdicts are ad-hoc. |
| **0–39** | Emerging tech is chased on vendor timeline or personal enthusiasm. |

### 3.2 Grader questions

- Where is the protocol?
- Where are last year's evaluations archived?
- What stops the same technology re-entering the pipeline every
  quarter?

### 3.3 Common down-marks

- Continuous scoring (down-mark to at most 70) — invites
  averaging and rounding-up.
- No refusal window (down-mark to at most 70).
- No archive (down-mark to at most 65).

---

## Dimension 4 — Portfolio governance (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Is the Innovation Portfolio
Board's authority scoped, binding on the classes named, with
dissent recorded and independence tested?

### 4.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | IPB chartered as an artifact. Composition fixed-role. External members with independence tested annually. Binding authority scoped (entries / exits / gates / horizon allocation / partnership templates / IP arguments). Advisory-only classes named. Dissent recorded with thresholds. Escalation defined to Board Strategy Committee and Audit Committee. Annual effectiveness review. |
| **75–89** | Board chartered. Composition and authority mostly defined. Dissent recorded but thresholds informal. |
| **60–74** | Board described but as "steering committee" — advises, does not decide. Authority not scoped. |
| **40–59** | Board is aspirational — meets when it can, no authority defined. |
| **0–39** | No governance body. AI Lab Director is the decision-maker by default. |

### 4.2 Grader questions

- Is the Board binding on anything?
- Is dissent recorded?
- Does independence get tested?
- What happens if the Board disagrees with the Lab Director?

### 4.3 Common down-marks

- Steering-committee framing (down-mark to at most 65).
- No external members (down-mark to at most 70).
- No dissent recording (down-mark to at most 65).
- No independence test (down-mark to at most 75).

---

## Dimension 5 — Metrics resistance to gaming (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Do the headline metrics reward
value transferred and options created? Is the receiving team
(not the Lab) the value source? Are known-gameable metrics
kept off the headline?

### 5.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Small headline scorecard (five or fewer). Transfer volume, transfer acceptance rate, option value created, capability built, horizon adherence — or defensible equivalents. Value estimated by receiving team. Gameable metrics (PoC count, papers, patent count, media mentions) explicitly refused at headline; reported as hygiene at most. Definition change is a governance artifact. |
| **75–89** | Scorecard is small and value-transfer-oriented. One or two gameable metrics still on headline. |
| **60–74** | Scorecard mixes transfer and activity metrics. Gaming resistance not explicit. |
| **40–59** | Scorecard is activity-count-heavy — PoCs, patents, publications lead. |
| **0–39** | No scorecard, or scorecard is "leading indicators" of activity. |

### 5.2 Grader questions

- What does the receiving team estimate, and how?
- What is deliberately not counted, and why?
- Who owns the definition of each metric?

### 5.3 Common down-marks

- Patent count as headline (down-mark to at most 65).
- Publication count as headline (down-mark to at most 65).
- Lab estimates its own value (down-mark to at most 70).
- Scorecard exceeds seven top-line metrics (down-mark to at
  most 75).

---

## Dimension 6 — Partnership and IP strategy (weight: 15%)

**What the grader is testing.** Do partnerships have written
contracts with defined exits, and are IP filings backed by
written strategic arguments?

### 6.1 Scoring bands

| Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Partnership templates (university / consortium / start-up / talent) named and structured. Contracts specify scope, IP flow, publication window, data-handling, named-successor logic, exit provisions. IP filing decisions require a written one-page strategic argument (defensive perimeter, offensive value, trade-secret alternative, cost/jurisdiction, owner). Freedom-to-operate cadence defined per product line. Standards-essential-patent posture stated. Open-source posture stated. |
| **75–89** | Templates and IP argument process defined. Some elements missing (for example, no named-successor logic, no FTO cadence). |
| **60–74** | Partnerships described in prose but no template. IP filed by count with no argument process. |
| **40–59** | Partnerships are "photographs" — announcements without scope, PI-successor, or exit. |
| **0–39** | No partnership model; IP is filed by whoever thought of it. |

### 6.2 Grader questions

- Can the grader find the four templates?
- Where is the strategic argument for a recent filing?
- What happens when a PI leaves mid-contract?
- What is the freedom-to-operate cadence?

### 6.3 Common down-marks

- No named-successor logic (down-mark to at most 70).
- Open-ended partnership terms (down-mark to at most 65).
- IP by count, not by argument (down-mark to at most 65).
- No FTO cadence (down-mark to at most 70).

## Dimension weights and aggregation

| # | Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Portfolio thesis defensibility | 20% |
| 2 | Transfer contract enforceability | 20% |
| 3 | Emerging-technology evaluation | 15% |
| 4 | Portfolio governance | 15% |
| 5 | Metrics resistance to gaming | 15% |
| 6 | Partnership and IP strategy | 15% |
| **Total** | | **100%** |

**Aggregation.** Weighted sum of the six dimension scores.

**Overall bands.**

| Overall score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **90–100** | Ready for Board Strategy Committee ratification as-is. |
| **80–89** | Ready with minor edits; a senior architect would sign it out after one review pass. |
| **70–79** | Solid draft; a second review pass required. Named gaps but no structural failures. |
| **60–69** | Directional but structurally incomplete. Requires a rewrite of one or two dimensions. |
| **Below 60** | Fundamentally not ready. Rework required. |

**Non-passing thresholds.** A submission scoring below 60 on
either Dimension 1 or Dimension 2 fails the assessment overall,
regardless of scores on the other dimensions. A senior architect
who cannot show either "what we are placing bets on" or "how
the bets exit the Lab" has not demonstrated the level.

## Grader notes

### What the rubric is not

- **It is not a scoring key.** A submission that structures its
  programme differently but defends the same properties can
  score at the top band. For example, a submission with two
  horizons rather than three can score full marks on Dimension
  1 if its thesis defends the two-horizon choice against three.
- **It is not a checklist for the reference programme.** A
  submission that reproduces the reference programme verbatim,
  without argued adaptation to its own scenario, scores lower
  than one that adapts sensibly.
- **It is not a preference for length.** Concise programmes
  that answer the six dimensions score above verbose
  programmes that hedge on all six.

### Reviewer discipline

- Grade against the rubric, not against personal preference for
  a specific innovation-management vendor or methodology.
- Distinguish "I would have done this differently" from "this
  is wrong". The former is a note; the latter is a down-mark.
- Where a submission adapts a reference component to its
  scenario, credit the adaptation.
- Where a submission omits a component the scenario would
  require (for example, an IP argument process at a company
  where IP obviously matters), down-mark rather than making
  the case for the learner.

### What to write in the review

- One paragraph per dimension: score, one sentence explaining
  the score, one to two sentences on the strongest and
  weakest points.
- Explicit call-out of any non-passing threshold hit.
- Named recommended next-review items in priority order.
- Do not rewrite the learner's programme in the review; the
  review is grader feedback, not co-authoring.
