# Innovation Portfolio Board — Charter

> Reference charter for the Halcyon AI Innovation Portfolio Board.
> Read alongside `../SOLUTION.md` and `../program/innovation-program-design.md`.
> The programme lives or dies on whether this Board has real
> decision authority. This charter is designed to give it that
> authority in scoped, defensible form.

**Version**: 1.0
**Status**: Reference charter
**Owner**: Board Strategy Committee (charter ratification); Group CTO
office (operational chair)

---

## 1. Purpose

The Innovation Portfolio Board (IPB) governs the AI Innovation
Programme's portfolio. Its purpose is to make and record
decisions about what the programme is working on, what it is
stopping, and what it is choosing not to work on — and to
protect the programme from three failure modes that innovation
programmes are documented to fail into:

- **Drift** — the portfolio quietly re-balances toward
  exploratory work without a decision.
- **Capture** — the Board defaults to approving whatever the
  Lab proposes, because no one wants to be the person who
  killed the exciting project.
- **Theatre** — the programme optimises for external visibility
  because external visibility is measurable and transfer is
  hard to measure.

The IPB is designed against those failure modes explicitly. Its
authority is scoped so it is neither a rubber stamp for the AI
Lab Director nor an operational engineering committee for the
projects.

## 2. Composition

### 2.1 Fixed-role seats

The IPB has the following fixed seats. Each seat is filled by a
role, not a person; when the incumbent rotates, the seat
transfers.

| Seat | Role |
|---|---|
| Chair | Group CTO or delegate at the CTO's level (Chief Technology Officer's direct report designated for this seat) |
| Product | Chief Product Officer or delegate at that level |
| Finance | Chief Financial Officer or delegate — typically the Head of Corporate FP&A |
| Legal / IP | General Counsel or delegate — typically the Head of IP |
| Lab | AI Lab Director |
| Product engineering | Product-BU engineering leader, rotating seat, 12-month term |
| External | Two external members — one industry practitioner, one academic. Nominated by the chair; approved by the Board Strategy Committee. Three-year staggered terms |

### 2.2 Standing invitees (non-voting)

The following are standing invitees. They attend, contribute
to discussion, and do not vote:

- Head of Internal Audit (or delegate) — for control observation.
- Group Chief Risk Officer (or delegate) — where the meeting
  agenda includes items with material risk-management overlap.
- Head of Human Resources for Engineering (or delegate) — when
  talent partnership or organisational items are on the agenda.

### 2.3 Independence

External members are independent of Halcyon in the year of
appointment and each subsequent year. Independence is tested
annually against these tests, following the disciplines used
for non-executive directors and adapted to this Board:

- No material commercial relationship between the external
  member (or their institution or firm) and Halcyon in the
  preceding twelve months, beyond the IPB honorarium.
- No family or personal relationship with a Halcyon executive.
- No direct or indirect ownership position in Halcyon above
  the disclosure threshold set by the Board Strategy Committee.
- No consulting engagement with a direct Halcyon competitor
  that would create a conflict of loyalty.

Independence failures are reported to the Chair and the Board
Strategy Committee. Loss of independence is not automatically
disqualifying — the Board Strategy Committee decides — but is
disclosed and recorded.

### 2.4 Conflicts of interest

Every member declares conflicts at seating and refreshes the
declaration annually. Specific conflicts (a partnership under
review involves the external member's home institution, an IP
argument involves a firm the Legal seat's family member works
for, and so on) are declared at the top of the relevant agenda
item and recorded in the minutes. Conflicted members recuse
from the specific item.

## 3. Authority

The IPB's authority is scoped. Two categories:

### 3.1 Binding authority

The IPB is the decision-maker on:

1. **Portfolio entries and exits.** Which projects enter the
   portfolio at G0, and which projects exit (transfer, park,
   or close) at any gate.
2. **Gate decisions G0–G3.** Continue, redirect with tighter
   scope, or stop, at each stage gate.
3. **Budget allocation across horizons within the Board
   Strategy Committee envelope.** The Board Strategy Committee
   sets the total programme envelope and horizon-share targets;
   the IPB allocates within that.
4. **Partnership templates and specific partnerships above the
   delegated threshold.** Reference default threshold: contracts
   over €500K committed annual value, contracts with named
   strategic partners, contracts with unusual IP terms.
5. **IP filing arguments, in batch.** The IPB reviews filing
   arguments quarterly and approves or holds them.
6. **Programme-level metric definition changes** below the
   thesis-refresh threshold. Changes that affect what is
   headlined to the Board Strategy Committee require Board
   Strategy Committee approval.

Binding decisions carry the record: dissent recorded (see
Section 4), follow-up committed, artifact-of-record archived.

### 3.2 Advisory authority

The IPB is advisory (not binding) on:

- Individual project technical direction. Technical decisions
  inside a project belong to the project lead, subject to the
  gate deliverables the IPB has approved.
- Receiving-team readiness. Whether a receiving product team is
  ready to accept a transfer is that team's own decision;
  refusal is recorded but not overturned by the IPB.
- Responsible AI evidence sufficiency. This sits under the
  Project 403 framework. The IPB can flag inadequacy at gate
  review but cannot substitute its judgement for the Project
  403 governance.

### 3.3 Escalation

The IPB escalates to the Board Strategy Committee on:

- Any proposed change to the programme thesis.
- Any proposed change to horizon-share targets.
- Any partnership above the delegated threshold or with unusual
  IP terms.
- Any project stopped after G3 with material sunk cost.
- Any IPB dissent above the recorded threshold (see Section 4).
- Any programme spend variance above threshold.

The IPB escalates to the Board Audit Committee on:

- Programme-level IP or freedom-to-operate risk that materially
  affects a shipping product line.
- Any external inquiry — regulator, standards body, litigation —
  naming programme output.
- Internal Audit finding on programme controls (raised through
  the Head of Internal Audit standing invitee).

## 4. Quorum, voting, and dissent

### 4.1 Quorum

Quorum is five of the seven voting seats, of which at least one
must be an external member and at least one must be Finance or
Legal. Meetings without quorum can be held but cannot make
binding decisions; binding items are carried to the next quorate
meeting.

### 4.2 Voting

Binding decisions are decided by simple majority of voting
members present, with the Chair's vote counted as one. In the
case of a tie, the Chair does not have a second casting vote;
the decision defaults to stop or hold rather than to continue,
consistent with the programme's default-to-stop discipline at
gates.

### 4.3 Dissent

Every dissenting vote is recorded by name in the minutes, with
the dissenter's stated reason if given. The reference charter
does not permit anonymous dissent — the IPB's decisions are
governance artifacts, and unattributable dissent undermines
their record.

**Dissent thresholds:**

- **Any dissent from an external member on a binding decision**
  triggers an entry in the standing report to the Board
  Strategy Committee at the next quarterly review.
- **Two or more dissents on any single binding decision**
  triggers an entry regardless of source.
- **Three or more dissents on any single binding decision**
  triggers immediate notification to the Chair of the Board
  Strategy Committee.

A unanimous IPB is reviewed by the Chair, not celebrated. Full
consensus on a portfolio decision is a signal — either the
decision was uncontroversial (fine), or the discussion did not
surface real disagreement (a Board hygiene problem worth
watching).

## 5. Cadence

### 5.1 Standing meetings

- **Monthly** — standing meeting. Standing agenda in Section 6.
  Gate decisions, portfolio review, standing exception review,
  standing partnership items.
- **Quarterly** — extended session with Board Strategy Committee
  liaison present. Scorecard review, horizon-share adherence,
  escalation batch.
- **Annually** — thesis review session. Programme thesis
  restated or updated for ratification by the Board Strategy
  Committee.

### 5.2 Ad-hoc meetings

The Chair may call an ad-hoc meeting on:

- An unscheduled emerging-technology candidate that a business-
  unit has requested urgent evaluation of (for example, a
  customer proposal contingent on an evaluation verdict).
- An external event materially affecting the portfolio (a
  regulatory move, a competitor announcement of consequence,
  a partnership emergency).
- An IPB escalation from the AI Lab Director or a rotating
  product-BU seat with a case for urgency.

Ad-hoc meetings follow the same quorum, voting, and dissent
rules as standing meetings.

### 5.3 Written procedure

For routine items — approval of a partnership under the
delegated threshold, ratification of a G0 with unanimous prior
support — decisions may be taken in written procedure by
circulation among voting members. Written-procedure decisions
still record dissent and are reported at the next standing
meeting.

## 6. Standing agenda

Every standing monthly meeting includes:

1. **Prior-meeting minutes ratification.** Corrections recorded.
2. **Portfolio state.** One-page state of the portfolio: count
   by horizon, count by gate, active count, closed since last,
   pending gate decisions.
3. **Gate decisions.** Each project at a gate this cycle is
   presented in its packet; the IPB decides continue / redirect
   / stop / transfer.
4. **New entries.** Candidates that have completed the Heilmeier
   evaluation since last meeting; verdict presented; IPB
   approves G0 or refuses.
5. **Emerging-technology scan.** Standing item. AI Lab Director
   presents the scan register and any changes. Parked entries
   are reviewed on their annual cadence in this slot.
6. **Partnership items.** Partnerships above the delegated
   threshold seeking approval. Partnerships under the threshold
   reported in batch for record.
7. **IP filing arguments.** Batched quarterly; standing report
   in intervening months.
8. **Exceptions and dissent register.** Standing item. Any open
   dissent-triggered items, any waivers, any exception counts.
9. **Metric scorecard.** Standing pointer to the current
   scorecard; deep-dive at quarterly session.
10. **Escalation batch.** Standing item. Any escalations to
    Board Strategy Committee or Board Audit Committee in the
    period, and any responses received.

Quarterly extended sessions add:

- Scorecard deep-dive (see Section 7).
- Horizon-share adherence review with Finance seat.
- Board Strategy Committee liaison items.

Annual thesis-review session:

- Structured review of the programme thesis against the last
  year's evidence.
- Recommendations to the Board Strategy Committee on thesis
  language, horizon-share targets, and metric definitions.
- Structured review of the IPB's own operating effectiveness
  (see Section 9).

## 7. Reporting

The IPB produces the following standing reports:

- **Monthly IPB minutes.** Distributed to voting members,
  standing invitees, and the Board Strategy Committee liaison.
- **Quarterly Board Strategy Committee pack.** Contains: state
  of the portfolio, scorecard (Section 7 of the programme
  design), horizon-share adherence, exception and dissent
  summary, escalations, upcoming thesis-affecting items.
- **Semi-annual Board Audit Committee pack.** Contains: control
  observations from Internal Audit, IP and FTO risk summary,
  external-inquiry summary, remediation status on any open
  findings.
- **Annual thesis-review report.** Contains: the current thesis
  language proposed for ratification, argued against last
  year's evidence; horizon-share target proposed for next year;
  metric-definition changes proposed.

Reports are produced by a scheduled job that reads from the
programme's own records (portfolio state from the gate register,
scorecard from the transfer register, exception and dissent
counts from the IPB minutes archive). Manual assembly of a
report is a signal that a record is missing a field; the
response is to fix the record, not the assembler.

## 8. Conflicts, whistleblowing, and separation

### 8.1 Conflicts

Handled per Section 2.4. Standing item on every agenda: "Any
conflicts to declare on the day's items?"

### 8.2 Whistleblowing and confidential concerns

Any voting member may bring a concern about the programme to
the IPB Chair in confidence. The Chair either handles it at the
IPB or escalates it to the Board Strategy Committee Chair. If
the concern involves the IPB Chair, it goes directly to the
Board Strategy Committee Chair.

External members have a standing invitation to raise concerns
directly with the Board Strategy Committee Chair without going
through the IPB Chair, as a check against IPB-level capture.

### 8.3 Separation from operational management

The IPB does not run projects. Project delivery and technical
direction stay with the R&D team leads. The IPB decides *at*
gates, not *between* them. This separation is the mechanism by
which the IPB retains the credibility to make hard decisions at
gates: it has not co-designed the projects it is now judging.

---

## 9. Board effectiveness review

The IPB's own operating effectiveness is reviewed annually by an
external party — a professional-services firm or an independent
expert appointed by the Board Strategy Committee. The review
covers:

- Attendance and quorum.
- Timeliness of gate decisions and portfolio actions.
- Dissent frequency and distribution.
- Consistency between IPB decisions and later outcomes (do
  approvals correlate with transfers?).
- Compliance with the charter (independence tests refreshed,
  conflicts declared, escalations on time).

Findings are reported to the Board Strategy Committee.

---

## 10. Charter amendment

This charter is amended by the Board Strategy Committee on the
IPB's recommendation, or on the Board Strategy Committee's own
motion. Amendments are recorded with the version, the effective
date, and the rationale.

**Minor amendments** (agenda item order, standing invitee list,
attendance recording format) may be adopted by the IPB itself
and reported to the Board Strategy Committee.

**Material amendments** (composition, authority, quorum, dissent
thresholds, escalation) require Board Strategy Committee
approval before taking effect.
