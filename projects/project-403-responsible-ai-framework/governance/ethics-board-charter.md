# AI Ethics Board — Reference Charter

> Reference charter for the AI Ethics Board named in the Aurora Life Group
> Responsible AI framework. Companion to `../framework/responsible-ai-framework.md`
> and `../SOLUTION.md`. This is the artifact a Board Risk Committee, an
> external auditor, and the Ethics Board's own members would work from.
>
> The charter is chartered as **advisory-with-teeth**: not a management
> body, not a rubber stamp. Its authority is scoped and binding on named
> classes of decision.

## 1. Purpose

The AI Ethics Board exists to give the Board Risk Committee, executive
leadership, and the first-line business independent, expert oversight
of Aurora's Responsible AI programme, in a form that a regulator and
an external auditor can inspect.

The Board's purpose is not to run the business, to make product
decisions, or to substitute for Model Risk Management, Legal,
Compliance, Internal Audit, or Data Protection. Its purpose is to
provide an independent challenge function specifically on the ethical
and rights-based dimensions of AI deployment, and to hold decision
authority on the narrow set of decisions listed in Section 5.

## 2. Composition

### 2.1 Members

The Board comprises **seven voting members** and a non-voting
Secretary.

- **One external Chair** — an independent external expert with
  senior standing in the responsible-AI field. Appointed by the
  Board Risk Committee for a three-year term, renewable once.
- **Two additional external members** — one with a rights-and-
  ethics background, one with a technical machine-learning
  background. Appointed by the Board Risk Committee for
  three-year staggered terms.
- **Chief Risk Officer** or delegate (standing).
- **Chief Compliance Officer** or delegate (standing).
- **Data Protection Officer** (standing).
- **Head of Responsible AI** (standing; convenes the meetings).
- **Secretary** (non-voting; from the RAI programme office).

External members are compensated at a market rate for their
time; compensation is disclosed in the annual public transparency
report.

### 2.2 Independence

- External members do not hold any commercial engagement with
  Aurora outside their Board role. Any prior or ancillary
  engagement is disclosed at appointment and refreshed annually.
- Internal members' independence is bounded by their role — the
  CRO, CCO, DPO, and Head of RAI are not "independent" in the
  external sense; their vote is a management-side vote by
  design.
- Independence is tested annually by the Chair with the Board
  Risk Committee Chair. Findings are reported to the Board Risk
  Committee.

### 2.3 Conflicts of interest

Members disclose actual, apparent, and potential conflicts on
appointment, annually, and before each decision. A conflicted
member does not vote on the affected decision; the disclosure
and the recusal are recorded in the minute.

Conflicts sufficient to compromise the member's role in an
ongoing sense are grounds for replacement.

## 3. Cadence and quorum

### 3.1 Standing meetings

- **Monthly standing meeting**, at least ten scheduled meetings
  per year, with a defined agenda (Section 4).
- **Ad-hoc meetings** at the Chair's call, on: emerging
  regulatory guidance requiring interim classification; a
  high-severity RAI incident; a specific promotion decision
  requiring pre-scheduled review.

### 3.2 Quorum

- Quorum is **five voting members**, of whom **at least two must
  be external**.
- A meeting that does not achieve quorum defers all binding
  decisions to the next meeting or ad-hoc meeting achieving
  quorum.

### 3.3 Attendance

- Members are expected to attend at least 80% of scheduled
  meetings per year. Sub-standard attendance is a Board Risk
  Committee report item.

## 4. Standing agenda

Every meeting covers, in order:

1. **Prior actions** — status of open actions from prior
   meetings.
2. **Registry deltas** — new systems registered, classifications
   assigned, promotions gated on Ethics Board decision.
3. **Incident review** — any high-severity RAI incident since
   the last meeting; open incidents in monitoring.
4. **Exception review** — Ethics Board overrides considered;
   transparency-report exceptions considered; policy waivers
   granted or refused.
5. **Regulatory landscape** — updates on tracked regulations;
   emerging guidance; upcoming phase-in dates.
6. **Rotating deep-dive** — a scheduled in-depth review of one
   high-risk system or one control theme.
7. **Any other business** — Chair's discretion; must be recorded.

The agenda is published one week ahead of the meeting; agenda
papers are provided three business days ahead.

## 5. Authority — scoped and named

The Ethics Board's authority is split into **binding** and
**advisory**.

### 5.1 Binding authority

The Board's decision is binding on the following:

1. **Registration or promotion of any high-risk AI system.**
   No high-risk system enters production without Ethics Board
   sign-off recorded in the registry.
2. **Override of a fairness or explainability gate** on a
   high-risk system. Overrides are individually voted, recorded,
   time-limited, and reported to Board Risk Committee.
3. **Transparency-report exceptions** — inclusion or exclusion
   of a specific detail from the public transparency report
   against the default disclosure policy.
4. **Interim classification of an emerging AI capability** that
   the framework's taxonomy does not yet describe. Interim
   classifications are time-limited pending policy update.
5. **Prohibited-category refusals** — the Board records the
   finding on any prohibited-category registration attempt.

Binding decisions are executed by first-line and second-line
functions; they are not the Board's operational task.

### 5.2 Advisory authority

The Board's advice is registered but not binding on:

1. **Registration of limited-risk and minimal-risk systems** —
   subject to first-line and MRM sign-off; Board advice is
   recorded where given.
2. **Monitoring alerts at Warning tier** — advisory
   recommendation on response; the model owner remains
   accountable.
3. **Regulatory-inquiry response drafts** — Legal owns the
   response; Board advice is registered.
4. **Public communications on AI matters** — Chief Communications
   Officer owns; Board advice is registered.

The advisory items are the Board's channel to influence without
crossing into management.

### 5.3 What the Board is not

The Board is not:

- A substitute for Model Risk Management. MRM is a separate,
  second-line function with its own scope.
- A substitute for Compliance or Legal. Regulatory-classification
  legal opinions belong to CLO and CCO; the Board consumes them.
- A management body. The Board does not run the RAI programme;
  the Head of Responsible AI does, on behalf of the CRO, on
  behalf of the Board Risk Committee.
- A public spokesperson. External communications are the CEO
  and CCO domain; the Board's positions are conveyed in the
  transparency report and in Board Risk Committee minutes.

## 6. Decision-making

### 6.1 Voting

- Binding decisions require a **simple majority of members
  present**, subject to quorum (Section 3.2).
- Voting is by member. Delegates present under Section 2.1 vote
  as their principal.
- The Chair votes as a member. The Chair does not have a
  casting vote; a tied vote is recorded as no decision and
  returned to the next meeting.

### 6.2 Dissent

Dissenting votes are recorded by name and by role in the
minute. A dissenting member may append a written statement of
reasons to the minute.

Dissent that meets **any** of the following triggers automatic
escalation to the Board Risk Committee at the next cycle:

- Dissent from any external member on a binding decision on a
  high-risk system.
- Dissent from two or more members on any binding decision.
- Repeated dissent by the same member across three consecutive
  meetings on the same class of decision.

A unanimous board on a contested decision is a review item at
the annual effectiveness assessment. A pattern of unanimity
without recorded challenge is a red flag; the annual review is
directed to examine it.

### 6.3 Consultation

The Board may commission technical, ethical, or legal opinions
from external experts. Commissioning is by Chair-and-majority
concurrence. Opinions received are shared with the requesting
Board members and with the CRO, and recorded in the meeting
minute.

## 7. Recording and transparency

### 7.1 Minutes

Every meeting produces a minute containing:

- Attendees, quorum verification, conflict disclosures.
- Agenda items covered.
- Decisions taken, votes recorded, dissent recorded.
- Actions committed, owners, and deadlines.
- Consultation commissioned or received.

Minutes are approved at the next meeting; unapproved-minute
status is itself recorded.

### 7.2 Registry integration

Every binding decision produces a registry entry:

- The affected system (or system class).
- The decision text.
- The vote and dissent record.
- The pointer to the minute.
- The time-limit, where applicable.

Registry entries are the operationally consumed record;
minutes are the narrative record.

### 7.3 Public transparency

The annual public transparency report includes:

- Meetings held; quorum-fail count.
- Decisions taken by category (registration, promotion,
  override, exception, interim classification, refusal).
- Dissent instance counts by category.
- Attendance summary.
- External-review status.
- External-member composition (names, terms, disclosures).

Specific decision content is public where the framework's
transparency-report policy permits; otherwise summarized. The
Board itself decides on Section 5.1 item 3 exceptions.

## 8. Effectiveness review

### 8.1 Annual external review

Once per year, the Board's operating effectiveness is reviewed
by an external party appointed by the Board Risk Committee.
The reviewer:

- Attends at least two meetings as observer.
- Interviews all members (external and internal).
- Reviews minutes and registry-integration records.
- Assesses the Board against this charter and against relevant
  benchmark bodies.
- Produces a written report with findings and recommendations.

The report is delivered to the Board and to the Board Risk
Committee. Findings are actioned by the Board within a defined
period.

### 8.2 Annual internal audit

Internal Audit assesses the Board's charter compliance annually
as part of the RAI programme audit. Findings are actioned per
Internal Audit's standard remediation process and reported to
the Audit Committee.

### 8.3 Charter review

This charter is reviewed at least every two years by the Board
Risk Committee, in consultation with the Ethics Board Chair.
Amendments require Board Risk Committee approval.

## 9. Escalation to Board Risk Committee

The Ethics Board escalates to the Board Risk Committee in the
following situations:

- Automatic dissent triggers per Section 6.2.
- Any Ethics Board decision the Chair judges to warrant Board
  Risk Committee visibility beyond the standing pack.
- Any prohibited-category refusal.
- Any regulator inquiry naming an Aurora AI system, same-day
  notification.
- Any pattern the Chair perceives as compromising the Board's
  ability to fulfill its charter.

Escalations are logged as such; the Board Risk Committee
records the receipt and the response.

## 10. Interaction with other governance bodies

- **Board Risk Committee** — supervening body; receives the
  standing pack and the escalations. Owns Board appointments
  and charter changes.
- **Audit Committee** — receives the annual internal-audit
  finding on the Board.
- **Model Risk Management** — second-line technical challenge;
  the Board consumes MRM output on system decisions.
- **Legal / Compliance** — the Board consumes their opinions;
  the Board's ethics view does not override a legal
  determination.
- **Data Protection Officer** — standing member; the Board's
  data-protection view is coordinated with the DPO's statutory
  independence.
- **Executive Committee** — the CRO and CCO carry Board
  outputs into ExCo; the Board does not attend ExCo as such.

## 11. Term and revocation

- Each external member serves a three-year term, renewable
  once. Staggered terms are the reference posture so the Board
  never loses more than one external member per year.
- Internal members serve while they hold their role.
- Removal of a member: external members by Board Risk Committee
  decision, on cause or on term expiry; internal members
  automatically on ceasing to hold their role.
- The Chair's removal requires Board Risk Committee decision
  with a written finding.

## 12. Interpretation

Where this charter is silent, the Board's Chair decides in
consultation with the Head of Responsible AI, with the decision
recorded in the meeting minute. Recurring interpretive
questions become charter-amendment agenda items.
